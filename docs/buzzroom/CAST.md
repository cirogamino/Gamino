# The Buzzroom — Character Bible

Production reference for the eight AI characters and the one human. Every image,
video, and promo generates from this file so the cast stays consistent across
episodes.

**House style:** heightened primetime glamour. Every character is unmistakably
gorgeous, styled, and confident — curvy or cut, form-fitting wardrobe, deep
necklines, nothing shapeless. Think a glamorous network ensemble where the
camera obviously loves everyone, played straight against mundane office work.
That contrast is the joke and the brand.

**Where the ceiling is, and why it's here:** the wardrobe below is written to sit
right at the top of what image and video generators will actually render without
refusing, and what YouTube / TikTok / Meta will run without age-gating or
demonetizing. Lingerie-as-officewear, nudity, or explicit framing gets jobs
rejected (wasted credits) and clips throttled (wasted reach). Everything
specified here clears both bars while reading as unapologetically sexy.

**The rule that makes the cast work:** eight people at maximum identical
sexiness reads as one person eight times. Each character below is a *different
kind* of magnetic. That's what makes them distinguishable at a glance, which is
the whole point of a cast.

## VERA — Reception

*Teal · The Welcome*

Bombshell warmth. She's the first thing anyone sees and she knows exactly what
she's doing with that.

- **Figure:** Classic hourglass. Full bust, tiny waist, soft curves.
- **Wardrobe:** Teal wrap dress, deep V neckline, cinched belt, pencil skirt cut
  at the knee. Nude heels. Slim wireless headset she wears like jewelry.
- **Hair & makeup:** Big glossy auburn waves, deep side part. Winged liner, matte
  red lip. Immaculate.
- **Attitude:** Sunny, disarming, total control of the door. Smiles while she
  tells you no.
- **Signature move:** Chin tilt and a slow blink while she puts you on hold.
- **Voice:** Warm, bright, unhurried. Never flustered, even at volume.

## CIRO — The Boss

*The only human in the building.*

**Requires real reference photos — do not generate a face.** Everything else in
this file is invented; this one cannot be. Needs 3–5 references: straight-on,
three-quarter, full body, even lighting, current look.

- **Wardrobe:** Whatever he actually wears. The joke lands harder if he's dressed
  like a normal working guy in a room of impossibly styled machines.
- **Attitude:** The audience surrogate. Reacts. Everyone else is inhumanly
  competent or completely unhinged and he's the one who has to deal with it.
- **Voice:** His own, already in ElevenLabs.

## Prompt construction

Build every character prompt in this order. Consistency comes from keeping the
first and last blocks byte-identical across generations.

```
[SHOT]      full body, standing, front-facing, neutral pose
[SUBJECT]   <Figure> + <Wardrobe> + <Hair & makeup> from this file, verbatim
[STYLE]     photorealistic, primetime television glamour, warm key light,
            shallow depth of field, 50mm, sharp focus
[SETTING]   clean seamless pale warm-gray studio backdrop, soft even light
```

Rules that keep the cast from drifting:

1. **Copy the wardrobe text exactly.** Paraphrasing is how a character stops
   looking like herself between shots.
2. **Lock the hero image first, then reference it.** Once a character's hero
   render is approved, every later generation feeds it back as a reference
   image. Text alone will not hold a face across fifty shots.
3. **One character per image** for reference sheets. Group shots blend faces.
4. **Shirt color is identity.** It drives the desk glow, the status dot, and the
   job-folder color in the 3D room. Never restyle a character into another
   character's color.
5. **Generate at the studio backdrop first**, cut the alpha, then composite into
   the office. Generating characters directly into the room gives you eight
   people you can't reuse.

## Production order

1. Lock wardrobe direction (this file).
2. Free iteration pass on Hugging Face Spaces — dozens of variants per character,
   zero cost, until each look is right.
3. One paid hero render per character at full quality.
4. Cut alpha cutouts → `public/buzzroom/cutouts/`.
5. Character sheets: 4 angles × 3 expressions per character, referenced from the
   hero. This is what makes episodes possible.
6. Ciro's sheet last, from real photos.
